I read `sequence.json` and all 30 images once each. I did not run the Python block at the end of your message, since you limited this review to the packet.

## What separates them
The lower body looks the same in both. Feet, legs and pelvis match in all three side details (0.00, 1.07 and 1.70 s), and the torso pitch looks close. **Floor support and leg weight therefore don't separate them**, so the verdict rests on the arms and sword.

**Shared lower body**
- **Strength:** the near-still stance gives way around 0.5 s to a deep forward lean, peaking near 1.0–1.1 s. The body then rises into a run by about 1.7 s, which reads as believable acceleration.
- **Strength:** in the three full-size details, the lead foot looks grounded and the rear foot is clearly lifted.
- **Weakness:** the first ~0.4 s is almost static, so the start is slightly delayed.

## Variant A
**Strengths**
- The low hip carry at 0.0 s suggests a heavy object.
- The near arm pumps naturally, and the near shoulder, elbow and wrist stay clean, with no visible armor collapse.

**Defects**
- **Not a two-handed hold:** the near hand is visibly off the sword in all three side details. It hangs free at 0.0 and 1.07 s and is an empty fist at 1.70 s. The sword appears to be held by the far hand only.
- **Weightless flailing:** the sword swings between tip-near-floor and vertical on each stride. It moves about 60° in 0.07–0.10 s around 0.87–0.93 s and 1.53–1.60 s, then whips back around 1.77–1.83 s. A blade this size should drag the body, but the torso and legs show no reaction.
- **Blade path:** the tip drops close to the floor around 0.67–0.73 s and again around 1.2–1.3 s. The blade also crosses in front of the legs, so a leg collision is possible.
- **Readability:** the blade fully covers the helmet in the side view at 1.70 s. This looks like occlusion rather than clipping, because the blade sits to one side of the head in the front view.

## Variant B
**Strengths**
- **Two-handed hold:** front and three-quarter views show the forearms converging on the grip at the chest throughout the run. This is the clearest advantage over A.
- **Controlled sword path:** the blade travels in one continuous arc, from tilted forward through vertical to a backward carry of about 35–45°, with only a small stride bob. There are no whips or reversals.
- **Clear of the floor and legs:** the raised carry removes A's floor and leg risks.

**Defects**
- **Near-shoulder deformation:** at 1.07 and 1.70 s, the pauldron rotates and crumples, and a dark gap opens at 1.70 s. This is B's most serious defect and could get worse on the game rig.
- **Stiff upper body:** the arms stay folded tightly against the chest, with little elbow or wrist change and no arm drive.
- **Too little weight:** a huge blade held high above the head causes almost no visible load, lag or counterbalance. It is much better than A here, but still too light.
- **Crowded head area:** at 1.07 s the blade appears to sprout from the top of the helmet in side view. At 1.70 s the grip passes behind the head. Front views suggest it clears the head, but only narrowly.

## Why B wins
B's problems are local: the shoulder, the stiffness and the too-light carry. A's problem is the action itself, because it is a one-handed swinging motion rather than a two-handed greatsword run. A's cleaner shoulder doesn't make up for that.

## Remaining uncertainty
- **Grip contact:** the sheets are too small to confirm finger contact. B's hands are hidden in the side view at 1.07 s, so its hold rests on the front and three-quarter views.
- **Far hand:** A's far-hand hold is inferred from the sword's position, not seen directly.
- **Clearance:** blade-to-helmet in B and blade-to-leg in A can't be confirmed from 2D images.
- **Foot sliding:** the camera appears to follow the character, so sliding can't be judged reliably.
- **Motion and game context:** these are sampled frames, so perceived rhythm in continuous playback is unproven, as are blending, the game rig and gameplay.

**Check first in the native trial:** B's near-shoulder deformation, both hands staying on the grip, and helmet clearance.
