## 3. Verdict on B as shown: **DO NOT SHIP**. **BOTH FAIL.**

A fails the brief outright, because it is a ruined cathedral. B is the right kind of place, but its finish isn't good enough to ship.

## 4. Version A on its own: fails

- **It's a church, which the brief rules out.** View 1 has a rose window, cathedral towers and a two-level arcade of pointed arches. Views 3, 5 and 8 have statues of hooded nuns or saints in niches. Views 1 and 8 have candle chandeliers, and views 7 and 8 have tracery windows. Nothing reads as a military fortress.
- **Chandeliers hang from nothing (view 1).** They are outdoors, and their chains go up into the open sky. In view 8 a chandelier's shadow lands on the flat backdrop behind the arches, which shows the backdrop is a flat plane.
- **Untextured blocks and floating geometry.** View 1 has a huge dark slab hanging over the top-right of the frame. View 7 has a black box sitting on top of the ruined window.
- **Clutter on the combat floor (views 1, 2, 8).** Carved shrine blocks are scattered across the court as obstacles. They break up the clear floor the brief asked for and make the space feel churchy.
- **No murals anywhere.** Views 3 to 6 show only dark stone. That stone is chunky, hand-painted and low-poly, which clashes with the realistic dragon and knight.
- **Lighting:**
  - Views 3 to 6 are almost black, so the surfaces can't be judged.
  - In view 8 the left half is lit orange and the right half cold blue, with a hard seam between them.
  - The dais in view 8 is a flat untextured slab.
  - In view 1, odd white streaks lie on the floor in front of the dragon.

## 5. Version B on its own: right concept, not good enough to ship

**What works:**
- It reads as a secular military gatehouse: crenellations, slit windows, iron bands, a portcullis.
- The court floor is open and uncluttered (views 1 and 2).
- The dragon and the player are both easy to read in view 1.
- The four murals are varied and look suitably aged: a military march, a fallen crown, a chained dragon and a star chart (views 3 to 6).

**Defects:**
- **The murals look like posters in display cases (views 3 to 6).** Each painting sits inside a riveted iron frame, with flat light-grey empty strips to its left and right. It isn't painted onto or set into the masonry. The paintings are lit evenly and brightly, much brighter than the stone around them, which makes them read as lit display panels. In the references, the murals fill whole wall bays under the battlements.
- **The murals don't appear in the wide shots.** No mural can be seen in views 1, 7 or 8. The only hint is a glowing orange disc at the right edge of view 1, which reads as a bright emissive object rather than aged paint.
- **The portcullis looks like a placeholder (views 1, 7, 8).** Its bars are flat, untextured ochre or brass, with no iron weight or rust.
- **Lighting artifacts in view 8.**
  - A hard-edged orange-red blotch covers the gate's lower wall and stops abruptly.
  - The dais is a flat, untextured blue-grey box.
  - The braziers are white chalice stands that look more like church furniture than military fire baskets.
- **Visible texture repetition.**
  - The stone walls in views 7 and 8 repeat the same tile pattern and look cold and evenly washed out.
  - The floor in view 2 shows an obvious repeating grid with streaky blue reflections.
- **Composition is thin compared with the references (view 1).**
  - There are no galleries, stairs, cliffs or distant towers to give depth.
  - The camera crops off the top of the keep.
  - A stray blue-lit sliver of tower sits at the left edge.
  - The braziers barely light anything. The court looks empty rather than menacing.
- **View 2 shows almost nothing.** It is top-down floor plus the dragon. The open floor is confirmed, but there's no architecture to judge.

## 7. Evidence limitations
- These are single still images. I didn't watch any motion, and I can't judge animation, gameplay or sound.
- B's views 3 to 6 are flat, front-on close-ups. They can't show how deep the mural frames sit or whether the art is actually set into the wall. The grey strips could be lighting or geometry; either way they read as a gap.
- None of B's wide shots show a mural in context, so I couldn't judge how the murals look across the whole court.
- A's views 3 to 6 are too dark to judge surface detail properly.
- The reference images are concept art. I used them only to judge the intended direction, not as a quality bar the game must match exactly.
