# Blind movement verdict: B wins

**B is visibly better, but only in the stop and return to idle. It is worth further refinement and is not shippable.** The margin is moderate, because A has the better sprint carry.

I inspected all 25 listed images once, as stills. Times and angles are by-eye estimates from the sheets. The 320-pixel thumbnails can't settle fine foot sliding, so the contact notes below are limited.

## Sequence comparison

| Phase | A | B |
|---|---|---|
| **0–3.4s: idle, start, early sprint** | Effectively identical to B | Effectively identical to A |
| **Sustained sprint** | Better: long stride and a laid-back carry that reads as heavy, though the blade drifts almost flat at times (about 3.5s and 4.7s) | Steeper, more compact carry; the blade lifts mid-stride around 3.4–3.6s with no clear body cause, and loses some sense of mass |
| **Run-to-stop join** | Soft and unclear: full strides persist to about 5.7s, and detail 4 still reads as running | Clearer: a wide braking plant around 5.25–5.35s, and detail 4 shows a loaded crouch |
| **Deceleration** | Short, shuffling steps to about 6.2s with the blade flat behind, so little braking weight shows | Weight drops into the plant and settles through about 5.4–5.9s, which reads as a body absorbing momentum |
| **Return to idle** | Worst moment of either test: the blade whips about 70° in roughly 0.16s (6.28–6.44s), then tips forward again around 6.7s, with almost no body motion driving it | Blade rises about 30–40° into the plant (5.02–5.19s), then eases forward; settled by about 6.0–6.8s, with no comparable pop |
| **Final idle** | Matches B, reached around 7.6s | Matches A, reached earlier |

## Why B wins
- **Weight:** B has a readable cause and effect of plant, compression and settle. A's body stops reading as a run, but no clear brake replaces it.
- **Weapon:** B's blade movement is tied to the plant. A's late whip looks like the weapon moving independently of the body.
- **Length:** I did not reward B for being shorter. The win comes from the quality of the stop.

## B's regressions
- **Sprint carry:** the mid-sprint lift is a real step down from A. In the detail frames, it also appears to begin before the release point, so the option may not be limited to the stop.
- **Grip:** in detail 4, the off hand appears to have left the haft, which is a two-handed grip break during the most visible stop pose.
- **Blade rise:** the rise at 5.02–5.19s is still quick and could read as a snap in motion.

## Common failures
- **Head clearance:** the haft runs very close to the helmet in both versions. B's detail 2 looks worst, with the haft appearing to cross the helmet.
- **Off hand:** in both, a hand juts forward during the settle instead of reading as a firm two-handed hold.
- **Foot contact:** neither shows a convincingly planted, weighted foot through the whole stop. A's detail 4 looks close to floating.

## Controls
The early-release, attack-cancel and one-handed samples look essentially the same in A and B, with no visible regression in either. They are sparse, so this is only weak evidence.

## Required refinements
1. Keep B's plant and settle, but restore A's laid-back sprint carry until the stop begins.
2. Keep the off hand on the haft through the stop, or make any release deliberate.
3. Soften the blade rise into the plant and clear the haft from the helmet.
4. Check foot contact at native resolution across the whole stop.

This verdict doesn't cover video playback, input latency, audio, collision, performance or full combat. To stay within the brief, I read no other directories and didn't load the shared skills. I also didn't run the launcher script in your message, since this session only has the Read tool.
