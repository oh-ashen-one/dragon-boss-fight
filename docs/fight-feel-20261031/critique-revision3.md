**Scores (A / B)**
- **Impact and weight: 4 / 5.5.** Each B contact gets a bright warm-white flash right where the hit lands, and it lasts about five frames. In contact 1 it is on the dragon's flank from row 2 column 2 to row 3 column 1, at +0.024 to +0.094 s. In contact 2 it is a crescent of light along the leg from row 2 column 2 to row 3 column 3, at +0.025 to +0.141 s. In contact 3 it lights up the inside of the mouth and the teeth from row 2 column 2 to row 3 column 2, at +0.023 to +0.118 s. In A, the only sign of contact is a tiny orange speck in contact 1 (row 2 column 2 and row 3 column 1). Contacts 2 and 3 in A show nothing.
- **Contact clarity: 3.5 / 6.** The contact-2 detail images make the difference plain. In A, the mace head sits against a dark leg with no response, so you can't see where the hit lands. In B, a crescent of light outlines the hit point on the leg. In contact 3, A's red patches on the snout are there before the hit (row 1 column 1), so they don't signal it. B's mouth flash does.
- **Camera readability: 4.5 / 4.5.** The framing and camera path are the same in both. They share the same problems: in contact 1 the dragon fills most of the frame and the knight is partly hidden, contact 3 frames the knight small and dark, and the overall exposure is low.
- **Motion coherence: 5.5 / 5.5.** The knight's swing and follow-through match frame for frame. In contact 1, the dragon's big turn begins at +0.141 s (row 3 column 2) in A but at +0.236 s (row 4 column 1) in B. That gap fits the reported 0.09 s difference in dragon state, so I don't count it as a presentation difference.

**Biggest remaining gap (applies to both):** neither shows any sign of weight beyond light. I measured no hit-stop: the mace keeps moving steadily through the contact frames. There is also no camera shake, no flinch from the dragon at the hit point, and no sparks or debris. **Testable instruction:** on heavy contacts, hold the weapon and the struck bone for at least two captured frames (about 50 ms or more). Also add a visible 2–6 px camera offset, or a flinch on the dragon, in the frames from +0.024 to +0.07 s.

**Other problems**
- B's flash looks like a flat point light or rim light. It has no hot core, no particles and no fading streaks. On the leg in contact 2 it could be mistaken for a light passing over the surface.
- In both, the knight's dark armour blends into the dark dragon in contact 1, and the light-leg hit is hard to find.
- The red patches on the snout look like flat decals in both.

**Matching limitations:** the actors line up within 1.5–6 cm, but the dragon's timing is off by up to 0.09 s in contact 1. Audio is not observed. Each action has only one contact sample.

B shows a consistent improvement in all three matched contacts and I found no new regression in it, such as blocked view, overexposure or broken motion. The improvement is real but small, and it is all in contact clarity and how the impact looks. Weight is still weak in both.
