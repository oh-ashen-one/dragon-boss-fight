VERDICT: TIE

**Temporal limit:** At about 12.5 fps there are roughly 80 ms between captured frames. A hit flash or trail that lasts 1–3 rendered frames can fall between captures and never appear. Per the manifest, contact should land around frame 6 of each sheet (0.4 s after the start; row 2, column 2). Contact-2 and contact-3 start at different times in A and B, so sampled phases differ slightly.

**Scores (A / B)**
- Impact/weight: 4 / 4.5
- Camera readability: 3 / 3
- Contact flash/trail clarity: 1 / 1.5 (unproven in both)
- Motion coherence: 5 / 5

**Evidence**
- **Contact-1 (A-contact-1, B-contact-1):** Both sheets are almost the same. The camera sits low and is mostly blocked by the dragon's wing and leg. The knight is a tiny figure at the bottom left in rows 3–4. Neither sheet shows the blade meeting the target or any flash or trail. In B at row 2, column 1, the dragon's foreleg near the knight looks somewhat brighter than in A at row 2, column 1. That may be a one-frame impact light, but I can't confirm it at this frame rate and resolution. The camera readability problem is the same in both.
- **Contact-2 (A-contact-2, B-contact-2):** In both, the camera looks up at the dragon's belly. The weapon shows as a pale lattice blade sweeping from upper left (A row 1, columns 1–2; B row 1, columns 1–3) to horizontal (A row 1, column 4 to row 2, column 1; B row 2, columns 3–4). Neither has a visible flash, spark or trail. The knight is almost hidden in both. This is a readability failure in both, with no difference between them.
- **Contact-3 (A-contact-3, B-contact-3):** This is the clearest window: a side view of the knight swinging at the dragon's head. Both show red patches on the snout (A row 1, column 1 to row 2, column 2; B row 1, columns 2–4 and row 2, column 1). B's patches are slightly bigger at row 1, columns 3–4. B at row 2, column 1 also shows a faint warm glow on the ground by the knight's feet, which A lacks. Both versions then have the same sudden camera jump between row 2, column 2 and row 2, column 3, from a side view to a front view of the dragon. Rows 3–4 are the same in both.

The differences that are visible are small, appear in only one frame each, and could come from sampling phase rather than real changes. No consistent advantage meets the "visibly better" bar. More red on the snout is not better feel by itself.

**Blocking defects (both A and B)**
1. In contact-1 and contact-2 the camera is blocked by the dragon's body, so the knight and the moment of contact can't be read.
2. The camera jumps abruptly between row 2, column 2 and row 2, column 3 in both contact-3 sheets.
3. Neither version shows a clear contact flash or swing trail in any sheet.

**Not proven**
- Hit-stop or hit-pause, camera shake, short flashes or trails shorter than about 80 ms, audio, animation blending between captured frames, and anything outside these three windows.
- Settling this needs captures of at least 60 fps that line up frame-for-frame on the same contact.

**Biggest gap to fix in both:** Recapture the three contacts at 60 fps or more, aligned to the same contact frame, with the camera framing the knight and the hit point unblocked. Then any flash, trail or hit-stop difference either shows up in at least 3 consecutive frames or is shown not to exist.
