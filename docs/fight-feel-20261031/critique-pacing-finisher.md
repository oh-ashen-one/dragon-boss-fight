# Blind finisher review — PASS A

Independent Claude Opus 5.5 high. Same Shipping build and 650 HP. Chronology, source notes and mapping withheld until verdict. Review input: uniform 2 s native timelines, 0.25 s ending sequences and final details, not continuous playback. Mapping revealed afterward: {"A": "final-quarter grounded fight and clear death shoulder", "B": "previously passed pacing without finisher refinements"}.

Scoped quality pass for the closing exchange and clear death presentation. Remaining notes: early aerial stretches, close dark attack readability, possible abruptness requiring continuous playback, and limited weapon visibility in the final pose. Whole-game and owner gameplay acceptance remain separate.

## Critic report (verbatim)

**Preferred: A**

**What I checked:** I read sequence.json, all seven timeline sheets, all six ending sheets, A-last and B-last. Health-bar percentages are my estimates of the bar's fill position on each tile (bar runs roughly x130–350). They are approximate, not pixel-exact counts.

**What both captures share (0–~62 s)**
- The first 62 s look nearly identical in both, so the same defects appear in both.
- **Health stalls:** health stays at 100% from 0 to ~20 s. It then stays around 85–87% from ~24 to ~38 s, so about 14 s pass with almost no visible progress.
- **Low readability near the dragon:** a dark knight stands against dark dragon legs at 26–30 s. At ~44–46 s, during the head-down punish window, the knight stands in front of the dragon's bloodied head and blocks it.
- **Camera pointed at the sky:** at 24–30 s the camera tilts up into the sky, and the dragon's torso fills the frame.

**A (82.2 s): evidence**
- **Steady, readable health loss after 62 s:** about 27% at 64 s, 20% at 66–68 s, 13–14% at 70–72 s, about 7% at 74 s, and empty by about 76 s. No stall here lasts longer than about 4 s.
- **Punish windows do the damage:** the head-down window at 46–54 s takes the bar from about 61% to 34%. The final head-down window (ending 73.1–75.6 s) delivers the kill.
- **Clear death:** at 75.85 s the dragon faces the camera head-up. At 76.1–76.6 s the head drops and the body rolls onto its side. From 76.9 to 82.1 s the dragon lies still with its chin and jaw clearly resting on the floor and blood visible. The knight stands to the right without overlapping it. A-last shows no obstruction, and the death pose is unambiguous.
- **Defects:**
  - The collapse happens in under about 0.5 s across the samples, so it may feel abrupt. I can't confirm that from samples.
  - After 76.6 s the large weapon head is barely visible; only a small round end shows beside the knight's hand in A-last.
  - The body behind the neck is vague.

**B (95.3 s): evidence**
- **Extra aerial phase:** health drops to about 13% by 66 s. From about 66 to 84 s it then shows little readable progress (about 13–14% to 7%, with inconsistent readings). That is roughly 16 s of tail-end stalling before the bar empties at about 86 s.
- **Camera problems during that phase:**
  - At 68–72 s the camera points into the sky, with the knight small or partly cut off and the dragon mostly out of frame.
  - At 78 s a pale, washed-out patch of dragon underside fills the top-right of the frame.
  - At 84 s the knight is cut off at the bottom edge under a huge neck.
- **Death sequence:** the rear-up at 87.2–88.9 s is shot from a low angle with the knight cut off at the bottom of the frame (ending 87.7–88.9 s). The fall happens at 89.2–89.9 s.
- **Final pose (90.2–95.2 s, B-last):**
  - The knight stands directly in front of the dragon's head and neck and hides the face.
  - The weapon head sinks into the dragon's chest and wing.
  - A thin grey spike runs from the wing to the floor and looks like a stretched-bone artifact.
  - The wing takes up about half the frame.
- **What B does better:** the head does rest on the ground, so ground contact is plausible, and the rear-up gives the fall a clearer windup than A's quick collapse.

**Biggest gap (applies to both; worse in B)**
- Remove any stretch longer than 8 s where the boss bar doesn't visibly drop.
- On the death camera, keep the dragon's head and the knight separated by at least about 10% of frame width, with no weapon–body overlap.
- B fails both through 66–84 s and in the final pose. A still fails the first part at 24–38 s.

**Secondary issues**
- Both: the knight blocks the dragon's head in punish windows (around 46–48 s and in the ending, A 73.6–75.6 s and B 85.2–86.2 s).
- A: the weapon is barely visible in the final pose.
- B: the camera tilts up into the sky during the aerial phase.

**Limitations**
- Samples are 2 s apart (0.25 s in the endings), so I can't judge motion smoothness, how abrupt A's collapse feels, or how either version plays.

**Verdict:** A paces the end of the fight better and gives a clearer death presentation with no obstruction. B's extra aerial phase adds length, camera obstruction and dead time without a matching gain in readable damage, and its final pose hides the dragon's head and shows the weapon clipping into the body.

**PASS A**
