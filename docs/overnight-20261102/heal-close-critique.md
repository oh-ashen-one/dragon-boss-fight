# Verdict: BOTH FAIL

B gets closer than A. It is the only one that visibly brings the cup to the visor. Neither is ready to ship: in both, the cup never tips to drink, and contact versus clipping can't be proven.

I reviewed all six sheets, 36 frames per variant. I only had a file viewer, so I couldn't crop or count pixels. Each frame is a tile about 640 px wide, so contact at the visor is judged by eye and stays unproven.

**Timing:** B lifts the cup sooner (at the visor by 0.40 s, versus A's hand reaching the face around 0.72 s) and holds it about 1.6 s, versus about 1.0 s for A. Both put the cup away at the same point.

## Biggest gap
Make the drink a real tip at the visor. From about 0.40 s to 1.60 s, the gauntlet should hold the cup by its handle with the rim at the visor slit, tilted at least 30–45° from upright. The forearm must not cover the cup in any frame from the observer camera. Then show contact with a full-size close crop of each frame, not 640 px tiles. The cup must touch the visor plate without passing into it, and must not drift away from the fingers by more than about 2 px.

## Other issues
1. **B entry:** the cup appears at 0.08 s, during the mace swing. Hold it back until the body has settled, as A does at about 0.24 s.
2. **Both, start:** the snap from overhead swing to standing in 0.16 s is too abrupt. It needs a real transition into the heal.
3. **Both, end:** recovery can't be judged because the camera framing jumps at 2.48–2.56 s. Re-record the end at a fixed camera position.
4. **Both, cup source:** the cup appears at the hip as a gold ring with no visible holder, flask or belt item. Where it comes from is unproven.
5. **Both, white flash:** a bright white triangle on the right pauldron from about 0.88 s on (A and B alike). It is probably a lighting highlight, but that is unconfirmed.

Neither should ship. If one has to move forward, keep B's lift and visor reach as the base, but it must not replace A until the tip and contact fixes above are done.
