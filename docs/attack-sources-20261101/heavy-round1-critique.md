# Heavy attack blind comparison, round 1

A = Kevin v7; B = existing heavy. Mapping disclosed after verdict. Relative PASS A; not shipping acceptance.

**Verdict: PASS A.** A is the clear relative winner. It is not proven ready to ship.

Evidence is six sheets of 16 evenly sampled frames each, named `A-view1/2/3.jpg` and `B-view1/2/3.jpg`. I had to guess the filenames because I couldn't list the folder, but I read no files outside it. In both candidates, frames 1–5 and 12–16 look the same as each other and are idle. The attack itself only appears in frames 6–11, so each trial gives about 6 attack samples per view, at low resolution and in dark lighting. I estimated by eye from the sheets and did not measure pixels.

**Weapon weight: A clearly ahead**
- **A:** The body visibly sinks under the weapon. A6 already shows a wide stance with bent knees and the blade raised (views 1–3). By A8 the knight is in a deep crouch with the torso leaning forward. In view 3 the blade tip angles down to the floor, and in view 2 it lies flat at ground level. A9 holds that low position, which reads as a committed slam that takes time to lift back out of.
- **B:** This is a horizontal sweep where the arms do almost all the work. In B7, views 2 and 3, the blade is fully extended sideways at chest height while the feet stay at idle width and the torso stays upright and square. Nothing in the body leans against the weight, so the weapon looks light.

**Whole-body coordination and silhouette: A ahead**
- **A:** The legs, hips and torso all take part. The silhouette shrinks to about two-thirds height at A8–A9 (view 1) and widens a lot, which reads clearly from all three angles.
- **B:** Only the torso turns, and only at B8. The silhouette change comes almost entirely from the blade. In side view (B7–B9) the body barely changes outline.

**Two-handed grip: can't tell for either**
In A8 (view 2) and B6 (view 3) the hands look close together near the hilt, but at this tile size I can't confirm both hands are on the grip, or rule out hands passing through the weapon. This is not proven for either candidate.

**Foot contacts**
- **A:** The stance changes from idle to a wide, staggered stance between A5 and A6. I can't see whether the feet step or slide in between.
- **B:** The feet barely change (B6–B7), so it shows little foot sliding but also little weight transfer.

Sampled frames can't prove foot sliding or planting either way.

**Anticipation, strike and recovery**
- **Anticipation:** Both have only one sampled frame (frame 6) before the strike, so neither proves a readable wind-up.
- **Strike:** A's peak (A8–A9) is legible. B's peak is spread across B7–B9 with no clear impact pose.
- **Recovery:** A rises over A10 (weapon diagonal, still crouched) and A11 (weapon vertical, knees still bent) and is back in idle by A12. B's recovery is shorter in distance because it never left an upright posture.

**Transitions into and out of idle**
Both jump from idle (frame 5) straight to a committed pose (frame 6). A has the larger jump, both into the crouch and back out: it goes from the A9 crouch to A12 idle in three samples, which may pop. I can't judge timing or smoothness from these frames, so both transitions are unverified.

**Ship readiness: neither is proven ready**
- **A:** Grip is unconfirmed, foot contact is unconfirmed, and the exit from the low crouch may be abrupt.
- **B:** It fails the heavy-weapon read outright, because the upright body doesn't react to the extended blade.

**Biggest gap, as a check for A:** In continuous video, or at least 30 sampled frames between A5 and A12, show that:
1. both hands stay on the grip, with no hand passing through the weapon, from wind-up through A11;
2. the planted foot stays put during the crouch (A6–A10);
3. the A9→A12 recovery has at least two in-between poses with no single-frame pose snap.

**Secondary issues**
- Re-render brighter or with rim lighting. The dark armour on dark ground hides hands and feet, especially in view 1.
- Neither candidate shows a held wind-up, so a heavy attack would benefit from at least one readable anticipation pose before frame 6.
- In A7 (view 1) the blade points up to screen-left. That fits the start of the swing arc, but I can't confirm the arc direction from one sample.

**Confidence:** High that A shows more weight and body involvement, because it's consistent across all three views in frames 6–10. Low on grip, contacts and transition quality.
