## Map art direction: gothic, explicitly not a church

Use a ruined, fortified seat of a fallen secular dynasty: an immense dragon-occupied war-court within a cliffside citadel. Gothic structure and oppressive grandeur should come from pointed defensive arches, massive buttresses, weathered towers, ironwork, portcullises, battlements, heavy gates, armory recesses, damaged galleries and monumental stone reliefs.

Replace the current cathedral reading in both composition and assets. Avoid a nave/central worship aisle, altar or apse focal point, pews, pulpits, crosses, saints, halos, devotional statues, biblical imagery and church-like stained glass. Removing religious symbols alone is insufficient if the space still reads as a cathedral. Existing pointed arches can remain where their proportions and context clearly serve fortress architecture.

Create deliberate large, medium and small forms: a strong fortress silhouette, major walls/gatehouse and galleries, then a limited set of readable architectural details. Use damaged masonry, soot, oxidized metal, worn paving and controlled debris. Choose a restrained charcoal/iron/stone palette, pale cold skylight and warm braziers or furnace light. Darkness must not hide the knight, weapon, dragon limbs, attack warnings or death pose.

Keep a generous, legible combat floor. Put bulky decoration and rubble around the perimeter. Preserve dragon movement/sweeps, player dodging, camera clearance, target visibility and clear approach routes. Measure required space from the real actors and attack footprints. Do not shrink the arena to make a prettier screenshot. Geometry must have useful collision and believable scale; avoid snagging, invisible walls, floor penetration, floating props and camera-blocking foreground clutter.
